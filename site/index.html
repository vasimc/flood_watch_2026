Caveat, not hidden: Sentinel-1's single operational satellite revisits any given spot roughly every 12 days. Assam happened to have a pass 3 days after its peak; Gujarat did not — the nearest usable pass was 10 days after Jul 24, after floodwaters had likely started receding. The pipeline widens its search window automatically when the requested one is empty (rather than silently returning a false "flooded = 0"), and flags it: Gujarat's number here is a real measurement, but very likely an undercount of the actual peak flood extent. Assam's number does not have this caveat.

Why it flooded

Assam, cited

Rainfall alone doesn't cause a flood of this size — it's rainfall hitting a landscape that's already been made more vulnerable. Both threads below are sourced, not synthesized from the rainfall data itself.

Natural

  • A cloudburst over Mon district, Nagaland (Jul 19) sent tributaries Dikhow and Dhansiri into sudden surge.
  • Those tributaries peaked before the Brahmaputra itself did — a timing "bottleneck" that compounds the flood rather than spreading it out.
  • Decades of sediment load have measurably weakened the Brahmaputra's own embankments.
source: Down To Earth, "Assam floods 2026"

Human-contributed

  • Deforestation upstream reduces the catchment's ability to slow runoff.
  • Mining and unplanned development further destabilize slopes and drainage.
  • Wetland loss removes natural floodwater absorption that used to buffer the plains.
  • Taken together, these upstream pressures are making floods in the region more severe and harder to predict, not just more frequent.
source: Down To Earth, "Assam floods 2026" (facts paraphrased, not quoted)

Is this getting worse?

2000–2025 · IMD gridded rainfall

A proxy for flood risk, not a verified flood-event count — real river-gauge history (CWC/WRIS) isn't open, so this counts monsoon days (Jun–Sep) each year where regional rainfall topped 2× the 26-year monsoon-day average, one bar per year. The two regions tell different stories.

Assam

Roughly flat, if anything slightly down (−0.07 extreme days/year, −5.6mm total monsoon rainfall/year over 26 years). 2026's disaster wasn't riding a rising rainfall baseline — it reads more like a single severe event landing on a landscape already weakened by the human factors in the section above.

Gujarat

A real upward trend (+0.27 extreme days/year, +9.0mm total monsoon rainfall/year over 26 years). Unlike Assam, Gujarat's 2026 event sits on top of monsoon rainfall that's genuinely intensifying — 2022, 2024, and 2025 are among the most extreme years on record in this 26-year window.

How to prepare next time

NDMA, CWC, ASDMA · cited, not a generic checklist

This section holds itself to the same rule as the rest of the site: every claim below traces to a named source, and where a natural follow-up question (did an official post-event review get published? does Gujarat have its own household checklist?) turned up nothing citable, that gap is stated rather than filled in with generic internet advice.

Real warning systems already exist

Neither region is starting from zero — the infrastructure below already ran during the 2026 event. The open question is lead time and reach, not whether a system exists at all.

CWC flood forecasting network

325 hydrological stations across 20 river systems and 25+ states and UTs, covering 197 towns/low-lying areas plus 128 reservoirs. Four alert tiers (Normal → Yellow → Orange → Red); Orange updates every 3 hours, Red hourly, with Red/Orange bulletins reaching the PMO directly. CWC reports >90% forecast accuracy and issues 7,000+ forecasts/advisories a year.

source: Central Water Commission
SACHET national alert app

NDMA's Common Alerting Protocol platform aggregates warnings from every authorized agency (IMD, CWC, state disaster authorities) and pushes location-specific alerts in 12 regional languages.

source: NDMA / SACHET
FLEWS — Assam-specific

Built by NESAC (Dept. of Space) for Assam, piloted 2009 in Lakhimpur, now covering every flood-prone district at revenue-circle level. Gives a 24–36 hour lead time with a reported 75% year-to-year alert success rate — explicitly documented as a planning tool, not for legal use.

source: NER-DRR / NESAC

What the 2026 response revealed

No official post-event institutional review has been published for either state as of mid-August 2026 — the event is too recent. What's citable instead is contemporaneous engineering commentary and reporting, labeled as exactly that below, not substituted for a government retrospective that doesn't exist yet.

Assam

  • Jorhat's embankments reportedly lacked proper drainage sluices — built to keep the Brahmaputra out, they ended up trapping water in once overtopped, prolonging inundation after river levels fell.
  • Upstream deforestation and mining in Nagaland increased siltation in tributaries like the Bhogdoi, narrowing channels and cutting discharge capacity.
  • Guwahati's ₹6,000 crore drainage master plan didn't prevent severe flooding elsewhere in the state — reporting frames it as an implementation gap, not a planning one.
sources: India Today NE (Nayan Sharma), Mahabahu.com

Gujarat

  • Floodwater in Navsari, Valsad, and Tapi got too deep for ground-based NDRF/SDRF teams — the IAF and Coast Guard shifted to airlifting people off submerged rooftops as the primary rescue method.
  • Reporting points to stormwater systems designed for outdated rainfall assumptions, plus encroachment on lakes, rivers, and natural drainage channels, as drivers of the urban flooding's severity — not rainfall volume alone.
sources: ThePrint, Counterview.net
Left out rather than guessed: a GSDMA-branded household checklist distinct from Gujarat's institutional-level SOPs (couldn't find one), and any single authoritative "lessons learned" document for either state (doesn't exist yet as of this writing).

What NDMA actually recommends

Quoted directly from NDMA's own guidance, not paraphrased into generic advice.

Before a flood
  • Avoid building in flood-prone areas unless the structure is elevated and reinforced.
  • Elevate the furnace, water heater, and electric panel if they're susceptible to flooding.
  • Install check valves in sewer traps to stop floodwater backing up into the drains.
  • Waterproof basement walls with sealing compounds to avoid seepage.
When a flood is likely
  • Listen to radio or television for information.
  • Learn whether you're in a flash-flood-prone spot (near streams, drainage channels, canyons) — if so, move to higher ground immediately, without waiting for evacuation instructions.
During evacuation
  • Secure the home first if there's time; move essential items to an upper floor; turn off utilities only if instructed to.
  • Never walk through moving water — six inches can knock you off your feet.
  • Never drive into a flooded road — a vehicle can be swept away; if water rises around a stopped vehicle, get out.

source: National Disaster Management Authority

How this was built

bronze / silver / gold Parquet · DuckDB · static JSON

All four sources flow through the same three real, persisted layers — bronze (the literal raw fact: an IMD grid cell, a Sentinel-1 pass, a GDELT count, a hand-transcribed quote), silver (one genuine derived step per source), gold (the story-ready table this site actually reads). Nothing here is relabeled for consistency's sake; each silver step below does real, different work.
